### What changed, and what was rejected

**B1: option (a), plus a precise disclosure of the residual.** Registration cannot be made atomic from Python. The audit event is raised inside `register_callback`, after its argument checks and before its exchange, and no API removes an audit hook or avoids the event. What revision 6 can do, and does:
- **Bound what an audit hook can cause.** Each exchange is gated on the name immediately before it, inside the same pipeline, so the gate and the exchange are separated only by that exchange's own hook. After the last exchange the owner is read again, still inside the consuming call. Nothing runs between an exchange and the next gate or the owner read, so exchanges 1 to n−1 landed on styxx's id, and only exchange n can have landed on another tool's, whose name the owner read gives. Revision 5's form could replace three of a foreign tool's callbacks in the critic's shape and report nothing; revision 6's replaces at most one and always reports it.
- **Act on the report.** A registration is *complete* only with five exchanges and styxx's name after the last. E4 uses an id only after a complete registration (else it moves to the next id or refuses MONITOR_BUSY); the reclaim counts only a complete one; X5 notes MONITOR_LOST for anything else. Every trace live at a split notes MONITOR_LOST too, because no exit's registration on a taken id can be complete until a reclaim or a rebinding has counted in `_LOST`.
- **Disclose exactly what remains** (L-MONITOR, M7 "The registration", #7, #20): one callback slot of the other tool is left as styxx's, and styxx does not restore it; a raising hook leaves a partial registration and propagates, out of `__exit__` at X5 (M5's heading, X157); the five audit events at every exit are a second site for the mutex's user-code cycle (#20, I5, "Audit hooks and slow owners").

Considered and rejected for B1:
- **Register once, at import or at the first `coverage_trace()`, and never again** (the critic's option (a) example). It moves the audit hooks out of later transactions, but X5's registration is the only detector of a replaced callback, since sys.monitoring has no callback getter, and a reclaim after an intermediate owner, or a rebinding, needs a registration anyway. Dropping X5's would make a replaced callback silent and unrepaired. Keeping the registration and bounding it removes no detector.
- **Restore the one replaced callback.** A restoring `register_callback` runs its own audit hook before its exchange, during which the other tool may register that event again; the restore would then replace the newer callback with the older. No bounded number of retries closes that, so styxx reports and does not restore.
- **Count a split in `_LOST` at the split.** Written in a first draft of this revision. It changes no record: every trace live at the split already notes MONITOR_LOST (above), and the model check's REGSILENT property holds with no such count (F21). It is not in the spec.
- **Verify "all five callbacks are exactly ours" after the call** (the critic's wording). There is no getter; a second registration to read them back would itself run five audit hooks. A complete registration already establishes that each exchange landed on styxx's id; a callback that a party replaces afterwards is the ordinary outside-party case, detected and repaired by the next exit's X5.

**M5: a patch-level list, not a runtime self-test.** The critic offered both. A runtime self-test at import or at the first `coverage_trace()` would need an audit hook to observe audit events inside the steps, and `sys.addaudithook` cannot be undone, so every traced process would carry styxx's hook for good. Revision 6 therefore restricts M0 to `_VERIFIED`, the patch levels on which the frozen dynamic gate G_ATOM passed with its controls failing, and makes G_ATOM a process gate. The cost is over-blocking #6's new line: every other 3.12 and 3.13 patch level refuses until someone runs G_ATOM on it.

### The revision-6 model check (M2)

`rev6/m6_modelcheck.py` keeps `rev5/m5_modelcheck.py`'s granularity and adds:
- **Registration with audit hooks.** `_register` is called at E4 (first acquisition, a reclaim of styxx's own freed id, an adoption, a rebinding), at the reconciliation's reclaim and at X5. Its five exchanges are abstracted to two, the entry and exit callbacks and the unwind callback, with a step boundary before each exchange where its audit hook runs. At that boundary a fault (a raising hook), a nested program, an outside event, another thread or a greenlet switch may run. Revision 5 gates once and reads nothing after; revision 6 gates each exchange and reads the owner after the last.
- **Callbacks and local events per id.** A hit counts only if its entry and its unwind reach styxx's callbacks on some id: local events and styxx's entry callback, and global PY_UNWIND and styxx's unwind callback. X5 notes a previous callback that was not styxx's, and a held mint without local events on the id.
- **Two ids.** `_ensure_tool` falls through to the other id when the one it holds is taken, with revision 6's count and local events (revision 5 has neither).
- **Outside parties, combined.** With re-entry (`ext+re`), greenlets (`ext+greenlet`) and `run_async` (`ext+async`); a kind may repeat (`free, reclaim, free`); two new kinds replace styxx's unwind callback or clear the mint's local events; `free` also applies to an id another tool holds.
- **Properties.** REGMULTI: more than one exchange of one registration lands on another tool's id. REGSILENT: at an end, a trace live when an exchange landed on another tool's id has a readable record without MONITOR_LOST. REGCLOB counts such exchanges (informational: the disclosed residual). CLOBBER counts every other styxx write on an id another tool holds, revision 5's registration exchanges included, since revision 5 claimed none.
- **The premise is an axiom.** `_unwind_on`, `_unwind_off`, `_take` and `_set_local` are one step each. The model's results hold only if CPython runs nothing inside them; G_ATOM tests that.

### Found while resolving (not raised by the critique)
- **F20. The process's first reconciliation raised TypeError.** Revision 5's reconciliation ended with `_unwind_off(None)` unconditionally. The first enter's E4 step 1 reconciles before `_ensure_tool` stores an id, so `_TOOL[0]` was None and the pipeline's `get_tool(None)` raised TypeError out of the transaction, so no tracer could ever be entered in a fresh process (`rev6/p_first_reconcile.py`, both versions). `rev5/mech5.py` fixed its tool id at 4 and never met this. Fixed by a guard (M3); the mutation audit names X36 as its witness.
- **F21. A count at a split registration has no observable effect.** A first draft of revision 6 incremented `_LOST` at a split whose owner is another tool. The mutant without it was undetected in the model and in every witness: while another tool holds the id, every exit's registration on it is incomplete, and the id becomes styxx's again only through a reclaim or a rebinding, which count. The rule is not in the spec (M7).
- **F22. The rebinding's count is necessary.** Without it, X137h's loss is silent: the blinded close found PY_UNWIND still set on the taken id, and P's exit registers completely on the new id (`rev6/w6_witnesses.py` X137h; the model's `rebind_count_none`).
- **F24. The `_LOST` count can land after another trace's exit.** A greenlet switch inside a reclaim (from its registration's audit hook, a finalizer or a signal handler) after the re-take and before the count makes the reclaim's owner look dead, so another greenlet's exit steals the mutex and runs its MONITOR_LOST test before the count lands. The model found it as the `blind_read_gated` mutant's LOSTNOTE; greenlet 3.5.6 reproduces it (`rev6/w6_greenlet.py`). ### Weakest points of revision 6, for round 7
1. **The registration residual.** One callback slot of another tool can be left as styxx's when an audit hook on `sys.monitoring.register_callback` lets that tool take styxx's id mid-registration. It is bounded to one slot, reported and noted, but not repaired, and the hook runs five times at every exit, inside the mutex.
2. **The premise gate's reach.** G_ATOM tests the one-call steps with the instruments, gc and signals it arms, in the gate outcomes it prepares. A CPython path that runs Python code only under conditions the harness does not create (a debugger that preempts C code, an allocator hook) would pass it. The model takes the premise as an axiom.
3. **Over-blocking by patch level.** Tracing refuses every 3.12 and 3.13 patch level except 3.12.3 and 3.13.12 until G_ATOM is run on it. That is most interpreters in the field.
4. **The binding check's strength.** It checks types, names and module identities. A C-level replacement that forges them, or a wrapper installed after the first binding (never called by the machinery, but also never detected), is outside it.
5. **The model's abstractions.** Two exchanges stand for five; greenlets and `run_async` are abstract; at most one nested program and at most three outside events run per configuration; counts are not modelled.
7. **Greenlets and the stolen mutex.** A greenlet switch inside a transaction still makes its owner look dead. Revision 6 found one consequence (F24) and keeps a detector for it, but only one real-greenlet shape is exercised (X137f); gevent is untested.
6. **The remaining cycle.** The mutex cycle through user code is bounded, not removed, and revision 6 shows it has a second trigger at every exit (a lock-taking audit hook on `register_callback`).
